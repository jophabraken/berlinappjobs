A law is coming. It probably won’t force the number into the ad.

Under the EU Pay Transparency Directive (2023/970), applicants have a right to the starting pay or its range: in the job ad, before the interview or in another way. Employers may no longer ask about your current salary.

  1. Jun 2023Directive (EU) 2023/970 enters into force
  2. 7 Jun 2026Deadline for national law. Germany misses it.
  3. Autumn 2026No German draft law published yet
  4. Expected 2027German law in force, date not confirmed

Not legal advice. Sources: EUR-Lex; Bundestag hib, 16 Jul 2026; Personalwirtschaft, 19 Jun 2026.

Method

All live job ads at the companies behind Germany's most-downloaded apps, read from their hiring systems (Greenhouse, Personio, Ashby, SmartRecruiters, Workday and career pages), as of 2026-10-10. We analysed 3,839 ads with full text, at most 60 per company. An ad counts when it names a concrete euro amount for pay: annual, monthly or hourly, a range or a minimum ("ab 2.700 € brutto"), or a salary field in the hiring system. Benefits, budgets and bonuses don't count, and neither does a reference to a collective agreement without an amount (another 151 ads). Detection is automatic and was checked by hand on samples. Ads without full text are not included.

FAQ

How many job ads state a salary?

At German app companies 11.7% of job ads state a salary, 7.5% for professional roles (as of 2026-10-10). Indeed puts all of Germany at 12.5% (May 2026).

Does the salary have to be in the job ad?

Not in Germany so far. The EU Pay Transparency Directive requires that applicants learn the starting pay or range, but also allows sharing it before the interview. In Austria a salary in job ads has been mandatory since 2011.

When does the pay transparency directive apply in Germany?

The deadline was 7 June 2026. Germany missed it, and a law is expected in 2027. No exact date has been set.
